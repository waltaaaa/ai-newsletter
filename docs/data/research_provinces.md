### Ontario FAO Economic Monitor — Q4 2025 (Released in Apr 10 window)

The Financial Accountability Office of Ontario (FAO) published its Ontario Economic Monitor covering July to December 2025. The Q4 2025 data represents the most recent sub-annual Ontario GDP intelligence available.

Key Q4 2025 findings:
- Ontario's economy was "little changed" in Q4 2025, after real GDP rose 0.5% in Q3 2025 (rebounding from -0.7% in Q2 2025).
- Employment increased 58,900 (+0.7%) in Q4 2025, the first strong quarter after two consecutive weak quarters.
- Ontario's unemployment rate edged down 0.2 pp to 7.6% in Q4 2025, the first quarterly drop in almost three years.
- International merchandise exports rose 7.6% in Q4 2025, following 1.5% growth in Q3. Metal and non-metallic mineral products drove the increase, boosted by gold prices.
- Ontario's goods exports to the U.S. fell 4.0% in full-year 2025, while exports to the rest of the world rose 17.0%.
- Full-year 2025 Ontario real GDP growth: estimated 1.3%, down from 1.6% in 2024.

## 5. Trade War Impact by Province

The U.S. tariff regime continues to affect provinces unevenly. Ontario and Quebec face effective tariff rates exceeding 6% on exports to the U.S., the highest among provinces, due to concentration in steel, aluminum, automotive, and softwood lumber sectors. Newfoundland and Labrador, New Brunswick, Alberta, Saskatchewan, and Prince Edward Island face rates below 1%.

Canada's merchandise exports to non-U.S. economies rose 17% YoY in the 12 months to January 2026, while exports to the U.S. fell 10%, indicating ongoing trade diversification. Ontario's goods exports to the U.S. fell 4.0% in 2025, while exports to the rest of the world rose 17.0%. Manitoba's nominal exports to the U.S. fell 18% YoY.

GDP growth in Ontario and Quebec is expected to be at the bottom of all provinces in 2026 due to concentrated tariff exposure.
