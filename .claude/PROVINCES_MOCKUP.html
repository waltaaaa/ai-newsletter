Provincial Analysis

Ontario's labour market added 5,200 jobs in March, a deceleration from the 18,400 recorded in February — the provincial unemployment rate rose to 6.4%, above the national average of 6.7% for the first time since September 2025.1 Goods-producing sectors shed 2,100 positions while services-producing industries added 7,300.

Cross-reference: The database tracks 287 active projects ($148B) in Ontario. Of those, 42 are in the construction sector — 8 list labour availability as a risk factor. The hiring signal monitor detected a +12% spike in construction postings across the Greater Toronto Area this week.

The province tabled its 2026-27 budget on March 27, committing $4.8B in new capital infrastructure spending — building permits in Ontario for February reached $4.2 billion, a 7.7% increase from January, driven primarily by non-residential permits in the Greater Golden Horseshoe.2

Consumer price inflation in Ontario registered 2.4% year-over-year in March, below the national average of 2.6% — shelter costs remained the primary driver at 4.6%, while transportation costs fell 0.8% year-over-year as gasoline prices declined.3

Provincial policy activity this week centred on a capital infrastructure budget commitment — and a land-use tribunal decision affecting residential development in the Greater Golden Horseshoe.

Ontario Budget 2026-27: $4.8B Capital Infrastructure Commitment
Ontario tabled its 2026-27 budget on March 27 with $4.8B in new capital infrastructure spending: $1.9B for highway construction, $1.2B for hospital expansions, $680M for transit, and the balance across education and broadband. 14 projects in the database are referenced by name. Ontario Land Planning Appeal Tribunal — Barrie South Urban Growth Ruling
The LPAT approved the Barrie South urban growth boundary expansion on March 26, enabling development on 420 hectares of land south of Highway 400. Two proposed residential projects ($680M combined) in the database are located within the expansion area. View on CanLII →
