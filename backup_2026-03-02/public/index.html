Overview

The EconF Weekly Newsletter is a weekly automated economic intelligence platform focused on Canadian structural transformation and capital investment activity. It is designed for strategic advisors, economists, policy analysts, infrastructure investors, and business leaders who need a rapid, high-fidelity view of Canada's economic landscape without manually aggregating data from dozens of sources.

The dashboard synthesizes data from primary government APIs (Bank of Canada, Statistics Canada), real-time commodity and equity markets (Yahoo Finance), government news RSS feeds across federal, provincial, and municipal levels, and AI-augmented research using Perplexity's Sonar Pro web search engine.

03

Methodology
Indicator Sourcing — Primary APIs First

All macroeconomic indicator values (rates, CPI, unemployment, GDP, commodity prices) are fetched directly from primary APIs before any AI calls run. These values are stored in Firestore and displayed on the dashboard. AI analysis reads those pre-fetched values — it never estimates, generates, or fills in numbers. If a primary source is unavailable, the cell displays Unavailable with a tooltip rather than showing a potentially incorrect estimate.

Data Provenance Badges

Every indicator cell shows a small coloured source tag so you always know where the number came from:

BoC Bank of Canada Valet API
StatCan Statistics Canada WDS
CMHC CMHC housing data
Yahoo Finance Real-time market data
ECB / BoE European central banks
Unavailable Primary source offline
Citations & Link Verification

All URLs embedded in provincial summaries and analysis sections come from Perplexity's Sonar Pro search results. Before publishing, each URL is tested with a HEAD request. Dead links (4xx/5xx responses) are replaced with plain text showing the publication name and date instead — broken links never appear on the dashboard.

AI Analysis Pipeline

The executive summary and province/industry analysis sections are written by Claude Sonnet. The model receives a structured prompt containing: (1) all verified primary-source indicator values, (2) Perplexity research summaries with citations, (3) recent government RSS headlines. Claude writes analysis about verified facts — it cannot fabricate data because the data is already locked in before the writing step begins.

Commodity Prices

Commodity prices are fetched from Yahoo Finance using mapped ticker symbols (e.g., CL=F for WTI crude, GC=F for gold, HG=F for copper, LBS=F for lumber). Each ticker is mapped to its World Bank Pink Sheet category and unit for standardized display.

Yield Curve Data

Government of Canada yield curve data is sourced from the Bank of Canada Valet API. The dashboard displays the full maturity spectrum from 3-month T-bills to 30-year bonds. The toggle pills let you select which maturities appear on the chart. The "Compare to" buttons fetch historical BoC Valet data for the same maturities at 1M, 3M, 6M, or 1Y prior — allowing visual inspection of yield curve shifts over time. A custom date picker is also available.

Project Tracker Methodology
GDP-Proportional Size Thresholds

Rather than applying the same dollar threshold to every province, the pipeline uses GDP-proportional thresholds. This ensures that a significant infrastructure project in Prince Edward Island receives the same research attention as a proportionally larger project in Ontario. A $10M hospital in Charlottetown is as strategically relevant as a $300M hospital in Toronto.

Project Discovery

Projects are discovered from two tiers: government registries first (free, verified, authoritative), then Perplexity research to fill gaps. RSS government feeds are also parsed by Claude Haiku to extract project announcements at no additional Perplexity cost.

Tier 1
Registries Impact Assessment Agency of Canada (IAAC), Infrastructure Canada project database, NRCan Major Projects Inventory, MERX (federal procurement), provincial environmental assessment registries (BC EAO, Ontario EA, Alberta AER, etc.)
Tier 2
Research Perplexity Sonar Pro queries targeting sector × province combinations (~31 weekly, ~260 monthly deep sweep) and RSS government feed parsing via Claude Haiku.
Deduplication

Before writing a new project to Firestore, the pipeline computes a normalized key from the project name and province (lowercase, stripped of punctuation and common stop words). If a matching key exists, the record is updated with fresh data — not duplicated. Projects are never deleted; they are only updated or marked stale.

Project Lifecycle
Announced → Approved → Under Construction → Completed
Cancelled Suspended ⚠ Status Unknown

The Status Unknown badge appears when a project has not appeared in any data source for 90+ days. It does not mean the project is cancelled — it means the pipeline has not found a recent update.

Classification — CMA, NAICS & Tags

Claude Haiku assigns each project a Census Metropolitan Area (CMA), a NAICS industry code, and descriptive tags (e.g., LNG, transit, renewable, affordable housing) based on the project description. These power the sector filter and search bar on the Projects tab.

Source Provenance Tags
federal_registry provincial_ea infrastructure_canada nrcan merx rss_feed perplexity_research

These tags are stored with each project record in Firestore and indicate how the project was first discovered.

05

Data Explorer

The Data Explorer (on the Markets & Yields tab) lets you overlay multiple time-series variables on a single interactive chart. Select any combination of macroeconomic variables — policy rates, CPI, unemployment, commodity prices, exchange rates — and compare their trajectories over custom time windows.

Time Range Options
1M, 3M, 6M, 1Y, 2Y, 5Y — chart filters to data points within the selected window.
Historical Accumulation
Each weekly pipeline run appends a data point. The chart becomes richer the longer the dashboard has been running.
Data Storage
All time-series points are stored in the /timeseries Firestore collection, keyed by variable ID and date.
Variable Coverage
BoC policy rate, CPI (national + provincial), unemployment, GDP growth, commodity prices, FX rates, yield curve maturities.

Things to Watch Methodology

The Things to Watch tab surfaces upcoming economic releases, central bank decisions, and policy announcements over the next 2–4 weeks. Entries are generated by AI from publicly available calendars.

Sources
Statistics Canada release calendar, Bank of Canada fixed announcement dates schedule, US Federal Reserve FOMC schedule, ECB Governing Council meeting calendar, Bank of Japan Policy Board schedule. All are publicly published.
Impact ratings
High — directly affects Canadian interest rates, employment levels, or GDP (e.g., BoC rate decision, Canadian CPI, Canadian jobs report)
Medium — affects Canadian financial markets or trade partners (e.g., US payrolls, Federal Reserve decision, ECB policy)
Monitoring — global context worth tracking (e.g., Bank of Japan, oil inventory data, Chinese PMI)

Update Schedule
Weekly Pipeline
Runs every week. Fetches all 23 RSS feeds, queries all primary APIs (BoC, StatCan, CMHC, Yahoo Finance, FRED, ECB, BoE), runs ~35 Perplexity research queries across all provinces and global macro context, generates full AI analysis, and publishes to Firebase Hosting + Firestore.
~35 Perplexity queries  ·  ~20 min runtime
Monthly Deep Sweep
Runs during the first week of each month. Executes an expanded NAICS sector × province research matrix: 20 NAICS codes × 13 provinces = 260 additional Perplexity queries for comprehensive project discovery. Also re-verifies existing project records against current sources.
~260 additional Perplexity queries  ·  ~90 min runtime

Limitations & Disclaimers

This dashboard is for informational purposes only. It does not constitute financial advice, investment advice, or any form of professional advisory service. Do not make investment or business decisions based solely on this content.

  • ·AI-written analysis and summaries may contain errors despite being grounded in verified primary-source data. The model may misinterpret economic signals or miss important context.
  • ·Provincial indicators sourced via research (grey badge) may lag behind official API figures by days or weeks, depending on Statistics Canada's publication schedule.
  • ·Historical chart data in the Data Explorer only begins from the dashboard's first pipeline run. Retrospective data is not backfilled from historical APIs.
  • ·Some government RSS feeds may be intermittently unavailable due to server maintenance. The pipeline handles failures gracefully, but some news items may be missed during outages.
  • ·Perplexity citations link to third-party sources. Content at those URLs may change, be corrected, or be removed after the edition is published.
  • ·Project valuations in the Projects Tracker are sourced from public announcements and may not reflect final approved budgets, cost overruns, or scope changes after the first report date.
  • ·Québec government news releases (RSS) are in French and may not be fully captured by English-language research queries. French content is processed without translation.
